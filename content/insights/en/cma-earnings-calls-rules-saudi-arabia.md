Every company listed on the Saudi Main Market must hold at least two earnings calls a year, starting with its FY2026 annual financial statements: one after it publishes its annual financial statements and one after it publishes its interim statements for the second quarter. The [Capital Market Authority (CMA)](@/insights/saudi-financial-regulators-guide/) circulated the requirement to Main Market companies on 19 November 2026, based on a resolution of the CMA Board. Each call must take place within five business days of publication and after the market closes, the CEO or CFO must be among the company's representatives, and the presentation and recording must be on the company's website within three hours of the call ending.

> **Key takeaways** The CMA's final provisions set a minimum of two calls a year, after the annual and Q2 interim statements. Each call falls within five business days of publication, after market close. If fewer than two financial analysts register within three business days, the company must invite its 30 largest shareholders. The CEO or CFO must attend, and the slides and recording go online within three hours. The first call follows the publication of FY2026 annual statements. As of 19 November 2026 the CMA had published the final terms in an Arabic press release only.

This guide is built from the CMA's own documents: the Arabic press release of 19 November 2026, which describes the final provisions, and the consultation draft of 29 September 2026. As of 19 November 2026, we did not find the text of the circular or the Board resolution itself on the CMA's website, and the press release had no English version. Where we quote the final terms, we are translating the CMA's Arabic, the only version the CMA has published. Read this as general information, not legal advice.

## What the CMA circulated on 19 November 2026

The CMA's press release describes the provisions as obliging all Main Market companies to hold "لقاءات لمناقشة النتائج المالية", meetings to discuss financial results. The draft called them meetings to discuss earnings. In practice both mean what investors call an earnings call.

| Requirement | Final provisions, per the CMA press release of 19 November 2026 |
|---|---|
| Who | All companies listed on the Main Market |
| How many calls | Twice a year at a minimum |
| When | First after publishing the annual financial statements; second after publishing the interim financial statements for Q2 |
| Deadline | Within five business days of the date the company publishes its financial statements |
| Time of day | After the market closes |
| Notice | Announced on the company's website, stating the means of holding the call and how to register |
| Low analyst interest | If at least two financial analysts have not registered within three business days, the company invites its 30 largest shareholders |
| Agenda | Financial results, guidance on upcoming financial performance, challenges and opportunities, and answers to attendees' questions |
| Who speaks for the company | The company's representatives must include, at a minimum, the CEO or the CFO |
| Publication | Presentation and recording on the company's website within three hours of the call ending |
| Start | With the publication of annual financial statements for FY2026 |

Source: CMA press release, 19 November 2026 (Arabic).

The CMA says the provisions aim to improve disclosure and transparency and to organise regular communication between listed companies, their investors and analysts about results and performance. It also says the rules should ensure that information is available fairly to all market participants. On our reading, the three-hour publication rule serves that second aim: investors who were not on the call get the same material within hours.

## How the final rules differ from the September draft

The CMA published the draft for public consultation on 29 September 2026, on the Istitlaa platform and its own website, for 30 calendar days ending on 18/05/1448H (29 October 2026). The press release says the circular follows that consultation. Comparing the Arabic draft with the final terms shows the following changes.

| Point | Draft, 29 September 2026 | Final, 19 November 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Number of calls | Twice a year | Twice a year at a minimum |
| Trigger | Announcement of annual results and of Q2 interim results | Publication of annual financial statements and of Q2 interim financial statements |
| Five-day clock runs from | The company's announcement of its results | The date the company publishes its financial statements |
| Slides and recording | Published on the website "immediately" | Published within three hours of the call ending |
| Analyst threshold | Not in the draft | Fewer than two analysts within three business days means inviting the 30 largest shareholders |
| CEO or CFO | Not in the draft | At least one of them must be among the company's representatives |
| Start | From the announcement of FY2026 annual results | With the publication of FY2026 annual financial statements |

Sources: CMA draft provisions (Arabic PDF) and CMA press release, 19 November 2026 (Arabic).

Four of these changes matter most for planning. "At a minimum" means two calls is a floor, and a company may hold more. The three-hour window replaces "immediately" with a fixed deadline that compliance teams can test against. The shareholder fallback and the CEO or CFO requirement are new obligations that the draft did not mention at all, so any preparation based on the draft alone will be short of them.

## Questions the published text leaves open

The press release is a summary. Until the full circular is available, several points that affect day-to-day compliance are unanswered:

- From when the three business days for analyst registration are counted.
- Who counts as a "financial analyst" for the two-analyst threshold.
- Whether the call may be held online, in person or both. The release says only that the company must state the means of holding it.
- What a company must do if the 30 largest shareholders are invited and few or none attend.
- Whether anything applies to companies listed outside the Main Market. Both the draft and the press release refer only to the Main Market.
- The consequences of missing a deadline.

If any of these points affects your plans, put the question to the CMA in writing and keep its reply on file. We will update this guide when the CMA publishes the circular or an English version.

## What the first calls mean for investor relations and compliance teams

For a company whose financial year ends on 31 December 2026, the first call follows the publication of the FY2026 annual financial statements in 2027. The second follows the Q2 interim statements later that year. Because the five-day clock starts on the date the statements are published, the call has to be planned alongside the results timetable.

The registration rule adds a second clock inside the first. The company must open registration, wait up to three business days for analysts (counting from the notice, which is the cautious reading until the circular says otherwise), and, if fewer than two have registered, invite its 30 largest shareholders in time for them to attend before the five-day window closes. A company that announces the call late can run out of days. The practical answer is to publish the call notice and registration route together with, or before, the financial statements.

The agenda goes beyond the numbers. The CMA lists guidance on upcoming financial performance, along with challenges and opportunities, as subjects the company discusses. Whatever is said on the call about the future becomes public within three hours, through the recording. Compliance teams should treat the script, the slides and likely questions as disclosure material and review them against the company's existing disclosure obligations before the call, with particular care that nothing material is said on the call that has not already been disclosed to the market.

Senior management time is the last constraint. With the CEO or CFO required on every call, both diaries need to hold the five business days after each financial statements publication date.

## Preparation checklist for the first calls

1. Fix the publication date of the FY2026 annual financial statements and block the following five business days for the call.
2. Confirm that the CEO, the CFO or both will attend, with a named alternate between the two.
3. Prepare the website notice: date and time after market close, the means of holding the call and the registration route.
4. Set up a registration process that records each registrant, their firm and the time they registered, so you can show whether the two-analyst threshold was met.
5. Decide in advance how you will identify your 30 largest shareholders on the day and how you will contact them, and draft that invitation now.
6. Write the call script and slides covering results, guidance, challenges and opportunities, and have them reviewed against your disclosure obligations.
7. Brief speakers on answering questions without disclosing material information that the market does not yet have.
8. Arrange recording, and test that the presentation and recording can be on the website within three hours of the call ending.
9. Keep an evidence file for each call: notice, registrations, any shareholder invitations, attendance, the recording and the time of publication.
10. Repeat the plan for the Q2 interim statements, and decide whether to hold more than the two required calls.
11. Watch the CMA site for the full circular or an English version and adjust the plan if the wording differs from the press release.
