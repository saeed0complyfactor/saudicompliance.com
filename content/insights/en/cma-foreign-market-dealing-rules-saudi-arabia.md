If your brokerage app lets Saudi clients buy US or other foreign shares, the Capital Market Authority (CMA) changed the rules on 29 October 2026. Circular No. 2613524 makes every capital market institution (CMI) assess suitability before a client starts dealing in a foreign market equivalent to the Main Market or the listed debt instruments market. That suspends the execution-only exemption that used to cover those trades.

The circular also bars the firm from using a client's property, information or opportunities for its own benefit or anyone else's, including its external broker's, unless it makes full disclosure and obtains express, written and separate client consent; that includes benefits from securities lending and payment for order flow. Margin trading abroad needs 50% up front and a 25% maintenance floor, and is barred on leveraged products and on securities linked to commodities, metals or volatility indices. The rules apply from the circular's date of issue, and firms have until 31 December 2026 to bring existing clients into line.

> **Key takeaways** The circular passes on a CMA Board decision of 28 October 2026 and has seven items: six set the rules and the seventh sets the start date and the 31 December 2026 deadline. Suitability is assessed once before foreign dealing starts and again only after a material change. Consent for lending and order-flow payments must be explicit, in writing and independent of other consents. Margin abroad needs 50% up front, daily monitoring and a 25% floor per position, and is barred on leveraged ETFs, other leveraged securities and securities linked to commodities, metals or volatility indices. The official text is Arabic; we found no English version on cma.gov.sa as of 26 November 2026.

## What Circular 2613524 says

The CMA sent the circular to capital market institutions and announced it the same day in an Arabic press release, which says the CMA had circulated the requirements to all CMIs. According to the press release, the circular follows the draft the CMA put out for public comment on 27 September 2026 for 30 calendar days on Istitlaa and the CMA website. As of 26 November 2026, Istitlaa shows the consultation closed on 27 October 2026 with 33 comments.

| Item | Detail |

| Instrument | CMA circular to capital market institutions, subject "dealing activity in financial markets outside the Kingdom" (ممارسة نشاط التعامل في الأسواق المالية خارج المملكة) |
| Number and date | 2613524, 29 October 2026 (18/05/1448H) |

The CMA issued Circular No. 2613523 on algorithmic trading on the same date; our [guide to the algorithmic trading cap](@/insights/cma-algorithmic-trading-rules-saudi-arabia/) covers it. A month earlier, on 30 September 2026, Circular No. 2613192 reminded CMIs of the suitability and written-consent rules for lending listed securities. The new circular carries those themes into foreign markets.

## Suitability now applies to execution-only foreign trading

Article 43 of the Capital Market Institutions Regulations (CMIR) bars a CMI from dealing for a client unless the transaction is suitable for that client. Paragraph (j)(1) lifted that duty where the CMI acts only as agent on the client's instructions, gives no advice, and the trade is executed in the listed debt instruments market, the Main Market or equivalent securities markets outside the Kingdom (Arabic: أو ما يعادلها من أسواق الأوراق المالية خارج المملكة). On our reading, that last phrase is the basis on which brokers could offer foreign shares execution-only.

Item 1 of the circular suspends paragraph (j)(1) insofar as it disapplies Article 43 to transactions in foreign securities markets equivalent to the listed debt instruments market or the Main Market. Firms must now meet the suitability requirements before they start dealing with a client in those markets. On our reading, the suspension is limited to the foreign part of the exemption; the circular says nothing about execution-only dealing on the Saudi Main Market or the listed debt market.

The circular also limits the workload. A CMI need not reassess suitability for each later transaction unless a material change occurs that calls for reassessment, judged on the facts the client discloses and any other facts relating to the client that the CMI knows or should reasonably know.

What Article 43 of the CMIR asks for in practice:

- An assessment against the eight factors in paragraph (b), including the client's knowledge and understanding of the securities, markets and risks, financial standing, investment objectives and investment horizon (the period over which the client expects to cash out).
- A suitability report given to the client before advising or effecting the transaction, covering at least the client's objectives and risk tolerance, the disadvantages and risks, and the reasons for the conclusion (paragraph (c)).
- A route for clients who insist on an unsuitable trade: the CMI may accept the order after notifying the client that it is inconsistent with the report and obtaining the client's acknowledgement (paragraphs (e) and (f)). In the Arabic text, the notification to the client (إبلاغ العميل) must be reviewed and approved by a registered person independent of whoever prepared the report (paragraph (g)).
- Technology-based suitability: paragraph (k) requires CMA approval, but a CMA circular of 27 July 2021 (No. 21/6028/6/1) exempts CMIs from that approval provided they notify the CMA in writing as soon as they start relying on the technology and put in place procedures and policies to verify the results.

For an app, this means a suitability step in the foreign-market onboarding flow, a stored report, and a trigger that reruns the assessment when the client's facts change.

## Consent for securities lending and payment for order flow

Item 2 reminds CMIs of their fiduciary duties under the CMIR, naming paragraph (3) of Annex 5.4. In our translation of item 2, a CMI must not use a client's property, information or opportunities for its own benefit or anyone else's, including the external broker, whether directly or indirectly. The exception is where it makes full disclosure and obtains the client's consent explicitly and in writing. Item 2 adds that the consent must be independent of any other consents that may be requested from the client, and that this includes benefits from lending securities and from payment for order flow.

Annex 5.4(3) on its own requires "full disclosure" and the client's "consent". The circular spells out that the consent must be express, written and separate, and it names the external broker. On our reading, a consent clause buried in general account-opening terms will not meet that standard.

Item 3 does the same for lending client securities under Article 86 of the CMIR when dealing abroad. Article 86 already requires express written consent before any lending, collateral covering the realisable value of the securities lent, daily monitoring of that collateral with top-ups unless otherwise agreed in writing, consent from every client whose securities sit in a pooled holding, and proper documentation. The circular adds that the lending consent must also be independent of other consents.

Items 4 and 5 apply Articles 42 and 43 when clients borrow securities or when client securities are lent in foreign markets. The CMI must take reasonable steps to make sure the client understands the risks of the type of transaction, having regard to the client's education and knowledge, field of work and capital-market experience, and must meet the suitability test for those transactions.

## Margin trading on foreign securities: 50%, 25% and a banned list

Item 6 extends Article 45's margin requirements to dealing abroad and adds three specific rules. The CMIR set the 50% and 25% figures for margin on "shares of companies listed on the exchange" (Arabic: الشركات المدرجة في السوق). The circular applies the same figures to a margin transaction on any security, or financing in any other form, in markets outside the Kingdom.

| Rule | Saudi-listed shares (CMIR Article 45) | Foreign markets (Circular 2613524, item 6) |
|---|---|---|
| Initial margin | At least 50% of the transaction value, before execution (45(c)(2)) | At least 50% of the transaction value, before execution, for margin on a security or financing in any other form |
| Maintenance | Daily monitoring; margin not below 25% of each position's current value (45(c)(3)) | Same: daily monitoring and a 25% floor per position |
| Products barred from margin | Derivative contracts (45(j)); shares of listed companies with accumulated losses of 50% or more of capital (45(i)) | Leveraged ETFs, any leveraged traded securities, and securities linked to commodities, metals and volatility indices |
| Through third parties | Not addressed in 45(c) | The product ban applies whether the CMI acts itself or through any other person, including the external broker; item 6(c) also requires the CMI to monitor margin when it trades through the external broker |
| CMA powers and data requests | The CMA may set higher margin or prohibit margin for any security or category (45(d), (e)) | Monitor margin, comply with decisions issued on it, and give the CMA data on request |

For illustration, a margin purchase of foreign shares worth SAR 100,000 needs at least SAR 50,000 of client margin before the order is executed. If the position's current value later falls to SAR 80,000, the client's margin must not drop below SAR 20,000. A leveraged ETF cannot be bought on margin at all.

Item 6 opens by requiring compliance with Article 45 when dealing abroad. On our reading, that brings in the rest of the article for foreign margin accounts too: written terms of business before any margined transaction, margin held in cash, fully paid securities or other acceptable collateral, and a list of securities eligible for margin that the CMI sets periodically under standards and controls approved by its board.

## What it means for investors buying US stocks from Saudi Arabia

If you trade US or other foreign shares through a CMA-licensed broker, expect a suitability questionnaire and report before you start dealing in foreign markets; existing accounts must be brought into line by 31 December 2026. If you trade on margin abroad, you will need to fund at least 50% of each purchase and keep margin at 25% or more of each position, and leveraged ETFs and commodity, metal or volatility-linked securities will not be available on margin. Expect separate written consent requests if your broker or its external broker lends your securities or receives payment for your order flow. The circular says the adjustment must not affect existing investment positions.

## Foreign-market provisions: draft and final text compared

Five differences between the September draft and the final circular matter for firms.

| Point | Draft (27 September 2026) | Final circular (29 October 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Start date | Margin rules to apply to margin transactions from 1 November 2026; the CMA's consultation notice said the final provisions would take effect from that date | Provisions apply from the date of issue; existing clients adjusted by 31 December 2026 |
| Loss-making foreign companies | Margin barred on shares of companies listed abroad with accumulated losses of 50% or more of capital | No such sub-item; item 6 has three sub-items |
| Suitability on lending | Item 5 covered clients borrowing securities only | Item 5 covers clients borrowing and lending of client securities |
| Fiduciary and lending items | Framed as "complying with" | Framed as the CMA confirming existing duties (التأكيد على) |
| Existing clients | Existing margin transactions handled without affecting current positions | All provisions: existing clients brought into line by 31 December 2026, without affecting existing investment positions |

Neither the circular nor the press release gives a reason for dropping the loss-making companies sub-item. The circular does not amend Article 45(i) of the CMIR, which bars margin on shares of listed companies with accumulated losses of half their capital or more. The Arabic text of 45(i) says "shares of listed companies" (أسهم الشركات المدرجة) without the "in the Market" wording used in 45(c), and item 6 tells firms to apply Article 45 when dealing abroad. On our reading, it is arguable that 45(i) reaches foreign-listed companies, so the cautious course is to keep them off margin until the CMA says otherwise; a firm can also exclude them when it sets its eligible securities under Article 45(h).

## Which brokers and clients are affected

The circular is addressed to all capital market institutions, and its substance bites on those that deal for clients in foreign markets. That includes app-based brokers offering US or other foreign shares through an external broker, firms that run foreign margin accounts, and any CMI whose external broker lends client securities or pays for order flow. On our reading, firms that only advise or arrange, or that deal only in Saudi markets, are not touched by the foreign-market items, though the reminders on Article 43, Article 86 and Annex 5.4 restate rules that already bind them.

Client-facing teams will feel it in onboarding. A client who opened a foreign account under the execution-only exemption now needs a suitability assessment before 31 December 2026, and some may be assessed as unsuitable for products they already trade. Article 43(e) lets a client proceed on an acknowledgement, and the circular says the adjustment must not affect existing investment positions.

## Broker checklist before 31 December 2026

1. List every foreign market and product you offer, and which of them you treat as equivalent to the Main Market or the listed debt instruments market.
2. Add an Article 43 suitability assessment and report to foreign-market onboarding, and run it for existing foreign-market clients before 31 December 2026.
3. Set a material-change trigger (profile updates, large changes in activity or holdings) that reruns suitability.
4. Build the Article 43(e) route for clients who insist: the notification that the trade is inconsistent with the report, reviewed and approved by an independent registered person, and the client's acknowledgement. Test it in your next [independent review](@/audit-review/).
5. Read your external broker agreement for securities lending, rebates and payment for order flow. Disclose each benefit and take separate, express, written client consent for it, or stop it.
6. For securities lending abroad, check collateral, daily monitoring, pooled-account consents and documentation against Article 86.
7. Reconfigure the margin engine for foreign positions: 50% initial, daily checks and a 25% floor, plus blocks on leveraged ETFs, leveraged securities and commodity, metal or volatility-linked securities. The blocks apply even where the external broker executes the margin trade.
8. Make sure margin data for foreign accounts can be produced for the CMA on request, and add these controls to your [ongoing compliance](@/ongoing-compliance/) monitoring plan.

## Status of the foreign-market dealing circular

As of 26 November 2026, Circular No. 2613524 is issued and in force from 29 October 2026, passing on the Board decision of 28 October 2026, and CMIs are inside the adjustment period for existing clients that ends on 31 December 2026. We found the circular only in Arabic. The CMIR's English translation is unofficial and the Arabic text governs; the version we read is the text amended by Board Resolution 2-3-2026 of 7 January 2026. We will update this guide if the CMA publishes an English version, amends the circular or folds these provisions into the CMIR.
