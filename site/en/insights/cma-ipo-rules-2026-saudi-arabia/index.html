The CMA's 2026 IPO rules: underwriting, orders and forecasts

CMA IPO rules 2026: the underwriting agreement must bind before book-building, orders need cash or cash equivalents, and prospectuses need forecasts.

Saudi Compliance editorial teamPublished: 2026-11-20Updated: 2026-11-2010 min read

On 2 November 2026 the Capital Market Authority announced that its Board had approved new regulatory provisions to enhance initial public offering (IPO) practices, effective from the date of their publication. Three things change. The underwriting agreement must be signed and in effect before book-building starts, and the underwriter's obligation to buy all offered shares runs from the first day of the book. Book-building orders must reflect liquidity the investor actually has, verified only through cash or cash equivalents, and must be binding for payment by the subscription payment deadline. And the issuer must disclose forward-looking statements and forecasts in the prospectus or registration document, with the financial adviser doing due diligence on them.

Key takeaways

The provisions are approved and, on the CMA's statement, apply from publication; the announcement is dated 2 November 2026, the same date the consultation notice had set. Underwriting risk now starts when the book opens, and if the underwriter ends up holding shares that breach the listing requirements, the shares are not listed and the underwriter still buys them all. On our reading, orders backed by credit lines or other non-cash comfort will not pass the new solvency check. Forecasts must cover the rest of the latest financial period in the document plus at least the following financial year. As of 19 November 2026, the CMA had not posted the promised FAQs or an updated text of the rules.

This guide covers issuers, financial advisers, underwriters and the capital market institutions (CMIs) that take book-building orders. It summarises regulator documents and is not legal advice.

The Arabic announcement says the provisions apply "ابتداءً من تاريخ نشرها", from the date of their publication. It does not give the Board resolution number or the date of the Board meeting. The consultation notice, in both languages, had said the final provisions would apply from 2 November 2026, which is also the date of the approval announcement.

Two gaps matter. First, as of 19 November 2026 the CMA had not linked a final text of the provisions from the announcement, and its pages for the Rules on the Offer of Securities and Continuing Obligations (OSCOs) and the Book Building Instructions still showed modified dates of 19 January 2026 and 28 September 2022. Second, the Istitlaa project page lists the consultation as finished and says its results and decisions report will be attached once the CMA has finalised its review. Until the CMA posts more, the announcement is the fullest public statement of what was approved.

Underwriting now starts when the book opens

Article 24(a) of the OSCOs already requires a public offer of securities to be "fully underwritten by one or more Capital Market Institutions licensed to carry out underwriting activities", each complying with the Prudential Rules. What moves is the timing. Paragraph (c) of item Third of the Book Building Instructions currently says the issuer must sign the underwriting agreement "before the beginning of the subscription process". Under the approved provisions, the agreement must be signed and effective before book-building begins, and the underwriter's commitment to buy all offered shares is effective from that moment.

The approval announcement also deals with a failed distribution. If the underwriter's ownership of the offered shares would breach the listing requirements in the Listing Rules, the issuer's shares are not listed, and the underwriter must still purchase all the offered shares. It adds a sentence that was not in the consultation draft: the underwriting agreement may include a mechanism for disposing of those shares.

Under Part 6 of the Book Building Instructions the subscription price may not exceed the price in the underwriting agreement, and under Part 4 any change to the price range needs the underwriter's consent and an updated underwriting commitment letter to the CMA.

Book-building orders must be backed by cash or cash equivalents

The Book Building Instructions already require the issuer's financial adviser and any other CMI involved in receiving bids to document them, check their validity, and verify the participating entities' solvency and ability to pay before registering them (item Third, paragraph (e)). The approved provisions tighten that check in three ways.

  • Each order must reflect the actual value of liquidity available to the investor.
  • Financial capacity may be verified only by cash or cash equivalents. The Arabic text says "على النقد أو شبه النقد فقط", cash or near-cash only; the English announcement says the acceptable means are limited to cash or cash equivalents.
  • Orders must be binding for payment no later than the subscription payment deadline in the prospectus timetable.

The consultation draft gave one example of a cash equivalent: securities convertible into cash during the subscription payment period set out in the prospectus. Three other draft proposals, listed in the comparison table below, are not repeated in the approval announcement.

Existing limits stay: the book may run for no more than 14 calendar days, and no participating entity may bid for 5% or more of the issuer's issued securities.

Prospectus forecasts become mandatory

The issuer must now disclose its forward-looking statements and forecasts, including forward-looking financial performance indicators, in the prospectus and the registration document. The approved period is the remainder of the latest financial period included in the document, plus at least the following financial year. The forecasts must be clear, not misleading, and based on reasonable and measurable grounds.

The CMA also points to clear disclosure of the forecasting methodology, the material assumptions, and the key risks that could cause actual results to differ.

The financial adviser, when advising the issuer on these forecasts, must exercise the necessary professional due diligence to reach a reasonable opinion on them. That sits on top of the adviser's existing duty under OSCOs Article 21 to satisfy itself, after due diligence and enquiry, that the issuer meets all conditions for registration and offer.

The draft linked this requirement to Article 27(b) of the OSCOs (the minimum contents of a share prospectus) and Article 82(a) and (b), which sit in Part 8 on the Parallel Market and cover the prospectus and the registration document. On our reading, the requirement therefore reaches Parallel Market filings as well as Main Market prospectuses; confirm against the final text.

What it means in practice

For issuers

  • Build the forecast before prospectus drafting starts: the stub period plus at least one full following year, with a written methodology, assumptions and risk factors.
  • Allow time in the timetable for the financial adviser to test the forecasts, because it must reach a reasonable opinion on them; on our reading that work has to be finished before the document is submitted.
  • Negotiate the underwriting agreement as a pre-launch document. It must be signed and in force before the book opens.

For financial advisers

  • Add a forecast workstream to your due diligence plan, with a file note showing how you reached a reasonable opinion.
  • Update your book-building procedures so that every order is checked against cash or cash equivalents before it is registered, and record the evidence.
  • Revise participation forms so that orders state they are binding for payment by the subscription payment deadline in the prospectus.

For underwriters

  • Price the commitment from the first day of book-building, and reflect it in your Prudential Rules capital planning, since Article 24(a) already requires underwriters to comply with those rules.
  • Agree the disposal mechanism in the underwriting agreement for shares you may have to keep if listing fails.

For CMIs receiving book-building orders

  • Stop accepting credit facilities, guarantees or similar comfort as proof of financial capacity. Under the approval announcement only cash or cash equivalents count.
  • Check discretionary-mandate clients under Part 5 of the Book Building Instructions. The liquidity test refers to the liquidity available to the investor, so on our reading the evidence must reach through to the client's own funds.

The roles themselves are licensed: the financial adviser must be authorised for arranging (OSCOs Article 21) and the underwriter licensed to carry out underwriting (Article 24). For a firm planning to add either activity, see the capital market institution licence guide.

Deals already in the pipeline

None of the sources we read contains a transitional provision. If your book-building has not started, plan on all three changes applying. If a prospectus is already with the CMA, or the book is already open, raise the point with the CMA case team now and get its view in writing. What to watch next

  • The CMA's FAQs for Implementing Regulations, where it said questions and answers on these provisions will appear. When we checked on 19 November 2026, the page showed a last-modified date of 2 December 2025.
  • A final text of the provisions, or amended versions of the OSCOs and the Book Building Instructions, on the CMA's regulations pages.
  • The Istitlaa results and decisions report for the consultation.

This guide reflects CMA documents as of 19 November 2026. We will update it when the CMA publishes the FAQs or a final text. For the wider set of CMA routes and the capital in force, see CMA authorisation.
